Nano Banana is a codename the Google developers gave to the 
image generation app for Gemini. Gemini flash 2.5 is the official name, 
but all the cool kids still call it Nano Banana. 
Nano Banana is an AI tool that uses plain text, called prompts, to 
create images. The more focused and descriptive your prompt is, the 
more likely you are to get the image you’re trying to create. 
Nano Banana is free to use, though free usage is limited. T o get 
started, go to gemini.google.com and sign up for a free account. The 
free account will give you about three to five free images each day. 
Each edit counts as an image so it’s pretty easy to burn through the 
free credits. A subscription is about $20/month. 
Creating product photos with Nano Banana is both magical and 
frustrating. When you nail it, the resulting image is a joy to behold. 
A few sentences and “Voilà!” a stunning image you can use for ads, 
emails, websites and social media. No photographer, no wait, just 
WOW! 
The flip side is, if you don’t nail it on the first go, it can be a hair 
pulling experience to get what you want. (I’m going to help you avoid 
that part.) 
I started my career as a professional photographer and for 15 years 
shot everything from china and sterling, to industrial shaker tables, 
food, and a lot of people smiling and shaking hands. I then switched 
to art direction and worked for and with companies like L.L. Bean, 
International Paper, Mrs. Beasleys, and Harrington’s of Vermont.  
AI generated images have fascinated me from the beginning and, even 
with a background in photography and art direction, it has not always 
gone according to plan. The good news is you don’t have to start from 
the beginning like I did, and once you understand the basics, you can 
get great results quickly.
In this guide I will walk you through how to structure your prompt, 
what to include, and how to reset when things don’t turn out the way 
you want them too.

The Core 5: 
What Nano Banana Needs
These are the non-negotiables. Without these, the AI has to guess, and it often guesses wrong.
AI can’t see the image in your head, so you must be specific about what you want, or you’ll get random results. This can 
be good, if you aren’t sure what you want and you don’t care about a specific outcome, or if you just want to get your 
creative juices flowing. Let’s say you want an image of someone looking at an overflowing closet with nothing to wear. 
The image just needs to look frustrating and a bit out of control. But if you want a specific image, you need to be specific 
about what you ask for. Being hyper-specific takes some getting used to, so when you start out, use the handy Perfect 
Prompt Worksheet at the end of this guide. Think through the shot and start with these five must have descriptions.
The Subject 
(What is it?)
If you’re creating a product shot, you need to start by 
saying you want to create a product shot and give Nano 
Banana a reference. Let’s say you want to show a new 
pair of slippers in a home situation, but all you have is the 
manufacturers shot of the slippers on a white background. 
(This is known as a silhouette, or silo.) Y ou need to give 
Nano Banana the silo as a reference, as well as specific 
instructions. The more specific you are the more likely you 
are to get the outcome you want. If you want a product 
shot with the slippers as the hero, you need to say that. If 
you want to show a woman lounging by a fire, you need to 
say that. The subject tells Nano Banana what to focus on.
The Environment 
(Where is it?)
A product floating in white space looks like a clip-art icon. 
A product in a “place” looks like a photograph. The more 
information you provide about where your product is, the 
closer the result will be to your expectations. 
Prompt Tip: “holding a cup of coffee in a sunlit kitchen” or even 
better, “woman holding a rustic mug of coffee in a cozy sunlit 
log cabin setting with mountains showing through the window 
and a Bernese Mountain Dog puppy sleeping at her feet.”
The Lighting 
(How is it lit?)
Lighting dictates the mood and realism. Bad lighting is the 
#1 reason AI images look “fake. If you want the product 
to look like it’s in a large store, you need to say that and 
specify overhead lighting. If you want it to look like it’s in 
a home environment specify soft lighting, window lighting, 
light falling from an overhead light with a spill of natural 
light from a window that is on the right but out of frame.
Prompt Tip: Keywords like “golden hour,” “soft window light” 
(for natural/organic products), or “studio neon lighting” (often 
used for tech/gaming products).
The Medium/Style 
(What kind of image is this?)
Nano Banana can create a 3D render, a watercolor 
painting, or a sketch. Y ou need to tell it to be a camera. 
Sounds super obvious, but to a machine that can do 
anything from anime, to a child’s drawing style, to 
impressionism, it’s a basic starting point. 
Prompt Tip:  For product photography use “High-resolution 
professional product photography”, “macro lens shot” (if 
you want a closeup), or “4k DSLR.” to get super clear, high 
resolution output.
The Composition 
(How is it framed?)
This controls where the product sits in the image. Imagine 
it your mind’s eye. Are you looking down on it? Are you 
looking straight at it? Y ou can tell Nano Banana how much 
or how little background to show in the image. T elling 
Nano Banana to “shoot from eye-level, crop in tight, and 
make the background out of focus” gets you the classic 
hero product shot. 
Prompt Tip: “Eye-level shot,” “top-down flat lay” (great for 
organizing items), or “close-up low angle.”

The “Pro” 5: The Secret Sauce
These details turn a “good” image into a “brand-ready” image. Once you get the hang of creating prompts with the 5 must-
haves we covered earlier, these five pro tips will help you really dial it in so they will work in different formats and will showcase 
your brand. Remember, while AI is amazing, it is also new to your project every time. Kind of like the movie 50 First Dates. It 
may seem like it remembers, but it doesn’t, so you will have to give it your brand colors and specifics every time. 
Do not give it your logo. It amazes me that it can take a product image and recreate it perfectly (though you always have to 
check) and yet it is very bad at adding a logo. I asked Nano Banana why that is and it said it doesn’t do a good job with text or 
specific graphic elements like logos. If you need to add a logo, it’s easy to create templates with layers in any design program, 
then just add a logo on a separate layer from the image. 
Mood & Vibe 
(T arget Customer)
This is where you use your target customer data. Think about 
who this image needs to connect with, outdoor adventures, 
sophisticates, active seniors, etc.
Instead of saying “for Gen Z,” describe the vibe that appeals 
to them: “Y2K aesthetic, vibrant pink and acid green colors, 
playful energy.”
Instead of “for professionals,” use: “sleek, minimalist, 
desaturated tones, organized.”
Y ou need to describe the environment that will be most 
attractive to your audience. Y ou can say “‘70’s groovy vibe 
with bold colors and curvy lines.” Don’t say “Like the artist 
Peter Max” That is a copyright infringement.
Negative Prompts 
(What to avoid)
Sometimes it helps to tell Nano Banana what not to do. It’s 
hard to think of what you don’t want in the first try at a new 
image. Nano Banana does a pretty good job of editing things 
out once you tell it what to take out. It’s worth thinking 
about obvious things you don’t want in the beginning.  
Example: “No text, no humans, no blurry background, no  
distortion.”
Aspect Ratio
(What size)
Nano Banana will output whatever it wants unless you tell 
it to give you a specific size. Because the output is in pixels 
it’s easier to provide an aspect ratio than it is to give it 
dimensions. Nano Banana does not do well with dimensions. 
Square images (1:1) are fine for Instagram, but terrible for a 
website banner. I have provided a cheat sheet for the most 
used aspect ratios at the end of this guide. 
Nano Banana outputs high-resolution images suitable for web 
and social media. Right-click to save, or use the download 
icon.
Tip: Specify “Wide 16:9 aspect ratio” for banners or “Tall 
9:16” for Stories/TikTok.
Brand Color Palette
(Y our colors)
Nano Banana can understand color codes or specific 
color names. If you have specific brand colors you should 
provide them as Hex codes ( # and six numbers or 
letters.) Canva or Adobe Express are good places to find 
the hex codes for specific colors.
Tip: “The background should be a soft pastel pink (Hex 
#FFD1DC)” or “featuring accents of electric blue.”
Reference Images
Y ou can often upload a photo of your actual product and 
ask Nano Banana to “place this product in a [scene].” 
Check carefully that the rendered image matches what 
you uploaded. AI hallucinates—meaning it invents details 
that weren’t in your reference—and makes up things. 
If it goes wrong the first time, start over and re-upload 
the image. It often helps to give the image a new name 
before uploading it the second time.  Limit the number 
of reference images you give Nano Banana. The more you 
give it, the more likely it is to make mistakes.
Tip: This is the most powerful tool for stores. It ensures the AI 
doesn’t hallucinate a different version of their product. Less is 
typically more, but experiment. 
Quick Summary
Think like a photographer, not a writer. T ell it what the 
camera sees (“soft light hitting a blue bottle on a beach”). 
Try to be specific and structured in how you describe the 
image you want. Avoid referring to famous artist’s work 
unless the work is over 120 years old, which puts it in the 
public domain. So don’t say “Make it look like a Richard 
Avedon image”, that’s copyright infringement and doesn’t 
respect the artist’s right to their work or their brand.

Let’s look at how actual prompts match output. For 
this example we’ll try to create an inspiring image of 
Air Jordans for a social media post.
Prompt 1
(T oo Vague)
Prompt text: “A photo of air jordans in a locker 
room.”
The Result: Nano Banana didn’t know this was 
supposed to be a product shot. Honestly, for a 
prompt with almost no information, it’s pretty 
impressive.
Prompt 2
(Good start)
Prompt text: “A pair of Nike Air Jordan men’s 
high top basketball sneakers,  [Subject]. In a busy 
locker room with used towels and athletic bags. 
[Environment]. overhead lighting with some window 
light coming from a large window on the right that 
is out of frame. [Lighting]. High-resolution macro 
product photography, sharp focus on the sneakers 
[Style/Medium]. Modern, professional vibe [Mood].”
The Result: Better than the first prompt. It looks 
like an Air Jordan product shot, it has a “vibe” and it 
could work for a social media post on how having the 
right gear helps you reach your full potential. 
Prompt 3
(Strong Product image)
Prompt text: “Professional styled product shot of 
A pair of Nike Air Jordan men’s high top basketball 
sneakers [Subject].  In a luxurious, tech-integrated, 
wellness-focused locker room with sleek, modern 
design and folded towels at the end of the bench.  
[Environment]. Soft hidden overhead lighting with 
some window light coming from a large window 
on the right that is out of frame. [Lighting]. High-
resolution macro product photography, sharp focus 
on the sneakers and the background blurred out. 
Make sure the sneakers are visually separated from 
the background and the sneakers take up most of 
the image. [Style/Medium]. Modern, clean, high end 
profession athlete [Mood].”
The Result: This elevates the sneakers. Depending 
on your target audience that may, or may not, be 
the right direction. Notice that Nano Banana kept 
the shoes the same size even though the prompt 
specifically said to make them take up most of the 
image. It also changed the aspect ratio to square.

How to recover when things go wrong
No matter what you do, no matter how carefully you prepare your prompt, at some 
point you will get a result that is not what you were looking for. 
After the initial prompt you can give additional instructions and, for some things, it can 
work really well. Say you have a product image and the handbag that randomly appeared 
in the background needs to go. Y ou can ask Nano Banana to remove the handbag and 
replace it with flowers. It might work perfectly. It might go very wrong. 
After giving Nano Banana instructions that add to the original prompt, if you aren’t happy, 
try changing one of the main characteristics of the image, like how the camera sees the 
image, the vibe or something fairly significant. If that fails, you’ll need to start over.
Starting over is just part of the game. T elling Nano Banana to reduce the size of a pattern 
on a fabric will get you nowhere. if it has removed a part of a lapel or added zippers to 
something a few times, the AI is fried. Before you start throwing things, tell it to start 
over. It also helps to change the name of the reference image— make a copy and add a 
v2 to the end will work. If Nano Banana gets stuck, it often can’t get itself unstuck. T ell 
it you want to start over, and change the prompt enough that it doesn’t keep using the 
original image it created as the starting point. 
The strength of Nano Banana’s image capabilities is that it magically creates amazing 
images with very little information, the weakness is that it often stumbles on specific  
iterations and small changes. 
If you get stuck, ask Nano Banana what to do. Seriously. One of the best ways to 
understand how to talk to AI is simply to ask questions. 
Nano Banana does not do retouching well. It can remove backgrounds and then replace 
them, but it doesn’t work well for fine tuning. If you need to retouch an image you will 
do better with Adobe Photoshop. Don’t focus on details that don’t matter. Remember 
that every photographer out there will shoot a different image of the same product, 
because every artist has a different vision. None of the big brands have matching images 
for every product. Don’t get caught up in the fine detail, just start over.
When to give up
If Nano Banana doesn’t nail it within three tries, it probably isn’t going to get it. Adjust 
the prompt and tell it to start over. If it’s really getting stuck on something and just 
can’t let go, you can start a new chat or wait a day or two and try it again. Nano 
Banana is a random image generator, so time does seem to change things. If you want 
the same look as a previous image, you should use that as a reference, but it may just 
limit what you get for output. Again—random image generator. The more you play, the 
better you’ll become. Given how AI works, there is always going to be some magic to 
the output that you can’t control. 
What the future holds
When PCs first came out, users had one floppy drive for the program and another for 
the project. Take a moment to revel in the wonder of how far we’ve come. Have fun 
with it. AI is in its infancy and it’s only going to get better. Y ou can’t break it, so learn 
with the fearless enthusiasm of a child.

Adding a Background
I was able to create a new shot of a coat that 
had been cropped tightly.  This is a good example 
of how to use Nano Banana to adjust styling for 
different target audiences. 
It took several tries but I was able to get the image 
I wanted by:
1. Editing the prompt to correct the mistakes.
2. Starting over when Nano Banana hit a wall. 
3. Adding a “2” to the reference image name and 
re-uploading it when I started over.
First Prompt
Use the reference image provided and create a photo 
showing the model wearing the jacket shown in the 
reference image, walking down the street in a quaint 
Maine village. Give the scene magic hour light. High 
resolution professional image. Show the model wearing 
white jeans, horizontal striped shirt and English 
riding boots. The model is happy and confident. 3:2 
vertical. Keep the colors cool and muted so they don’t 
overpower the jacket.
Final Prompt
Start over. Use the reference image provided and create a photo showing 
the model wearing the jacket shown in the reference image, it should 
have a wide continuous collar without a notch. The model should be 
walking down the street in a quaint Maine village. Give the scene magic 
hour light. High resolution professional image. Show the model wearing 
white jeans, horizontal striped shirt and English riding boots. The jacket 
will be below knee length and cover the top of the boots. The model is 
happy and confident. 3:2 vertical. Keep the colors cool and muted so 
they don’t overpower the jacket.
